Steel Plate Strengthening — Theory Notes

The models behind the strengthening module: flexural strengthening of RC beams with bonded or bolted steel plates, the shear flow and bolts at the plate–concrete interface, and steel side plates for shear, with the parts that are code and the parts that are engineering models.

⚠️ A calculation aid, not a substitute for engineering judgement. Results must be checked and accepted by the qualified engineer responsible for the design. Scope, assumptions and limitations →
Overview

Strengthening with steel plates adds steel to an existing RC beam: plates on the soffit (and sometimes the top) for flexure, and plates on the sides of the web for shear. The module follows one rule wherever it can: transform, then delegate. A plate is turned into an equivalent quantity that the rc module already knows (reinforcement area, a shear contribution) and the capacity is calculated by the same ACI 318-19 functions as an unstrengthened beam.

⚠️ ACI 318-19 has no provisions for steel plates bonded or bolted to concrete. Where this page uses ACI 318 clauses, they apply to the concrete and the anchors; the plate models themselves are engineering adaptations of AISC 360 and ACI 440.2R. Confirm them against test data or a specialist review before relying on them. The library says so in a warning on every side-plate result.

Assumptions
  • Full composite action. Plate and concrete act together up to failure; the bolts or the bond are designed separately (below) to make that true.
  • The plates yield. In flexure the plate is counted at its yield force, like reinforcement.
  • No initial load. The beam is assumed unloaded when the plates are fixed. In practice the dead load is already on the beam, so the existing steel is already strained when the plate starts to work; this is not modelled.
  • Sound existing structure. The existing concrete, reinforcement and cover are as specified, without corrosion or damage.
Plate jacketing for flexure rc-beam-steel-plate-jacketing.ts

A plate of width bp, thickness tp and yield strength fy,p develops the same force as a rebar area that is scaled by the strength ratio:

n = fy,p / fy,   Ap,eq = n · bp tpfy: yield strength of the existing reinforcement

Depths stay measured from the top face of the concrete, so the stress block of rectBeamMomentCapacity covers concrete only:

  • Bottom plate: its centroid is at h + tb/2. It is added to the tension steel, at the combined centroid
    dnew = d · As + (h + tb/2) · Ap,eqAs + Ap,eq,   As,new = As + Ap,eq
  • Top plate: its centroid is tt/2 above the concrete face, at d′ = −tt/2. It is added to the compression steel at the combined centroid, which turns a singly reinforced section into a doubly reinforced one.

The modified section is then passed to rectBeamMomentCapacity, so every check of the RC beam design applies, including the tension-control check. The result gives the capacity before and after strengthening.

Worked example
TypeScript
calculateSteelJacketedBeamMomentCapacity(
  { Es: 200000, fy: 400, fc_: 28, b: 300, h: 500, d: 440, As: 942 }, // MPa, mm, mm²
  { Es: 200000, fy: 250, bottomSteelWidth: 200, bottomSteelThickness: 6 },
);
// before.phiMn = 140.26 kN·m   (c: 62.09, a: 52.77)
// after.phiMn  = 256.15 kN·m   (As: 1692, d: 467.926, c: 111.52, a: 94.79)

By hand: n = 250/400 = 0.625, Ap,eq = 0.625 × 200 × 6 = 750 mm², dnew = (440 × 942 + 503 × 750)/1692 = 467.93 mm, a = 1692 × 400/(0.85 × 28 × 300) = 94.79 mm and φMn = 0.9 × 1692 × 400 × (467.93 − 47.40) = 256.15 kN·m.

⚠️ Throws RCDesignError 102 when the jacketed section is not tension-controlled. Adding plate steel raises the compression depth, so a section that passed before strengthening can fail after it: the same beam with As = 1520 mm² and a 250 × 8 mm plate throws 102.
Elastic transformed section rc-beam-plate-interface-bolts.ts

The interface design uses the elastic section at service-type behaviour, not the strength model above. Depths y are measured from a datum at the top of the top plate (the top face of the concrete when there is none). Steel is transformed to concrete with the modular ratios

ns = Es / Ec,   np = Ep / Ec,   Ec = 4700√f′c

Cracked section (the default): the neutral axis ȳ makes the first moment of the transformed areas zero, with only the concrete above ȳ counted:

½ b (ȳ − yc)² = Σi ni Ai ( yi − ȳ )yc: concrete top face; sum over plates and bars with their sign

Compression bars displace concrete that the block already counts, so they take (ns − 1)A′s. If they turn out to lie below ȳ, the equation is solved again with nsA′s and a warning citing ACI318-19, 22.2.2.1. The quadratic's root inside the concrete depth is taken; if there is none, RCDesignError 204 is thrown. Uncracked section: the gross concrete plus (ns − 1) times the bars and np times the plates, with ȳ at the centroid. In both cases

Itr = Σi [ Ii + Ai ( yi − ȳ )² ]
Interface shear flow

The horizontal shear per unit length at the interface between a plate and the concrete is

q = V · QItr,   Q = np Ap | yp − ȳ |Q: first moment of the plate beyond the interface

ȳ and Itr come from one solve with every plate present, so adding a top plate also changes the shear flow at the bottom interface.

TypeScript
plateInterfaceShearFlow({ section, plates, V: 150 }); // same beam and plate as above; V in kN
// bottom: { q: 220.32 N/mm, Q: 3086503 mm³, leverArm: 319.84 mm }, top: null
// calculationDetails: { Ec: 24870.06, ns: 8.042, np: 8.042, ybar: 183.16, Itr: 2101397661, sectionState: "cracked" }

By hand: 150ȳ² = 8.042 × 942 × (440 − ȳ) + 8.042 × 1200 × (503 − ȳ) gives ȳ = 183.16 mm; Q = 8.042 × 1200 × (503 − 183.16) = 3.087 × 10⁶ mm³, and q = 150 000 × 3.087 × 10⁶ / 2.101 × 10⁹ = 220.3 N/mm.

⚠️ Throws RCDesignError 201 when V ≤ 0 or there is no plate, 203 for inconsistent geometry, 204 when no cracked neutral axis lies within the concrete.
Anchor strength in shear
φVbolt = 0.65 · 0.6 · Ase,V · futa · nplanes,   futa ≤ min(1.9 fya, 860 MPa) ACI 318-19, 17.7.1.2(b), Table 17.5.3, 17.6.1.2

For Ase,V = 84.3 mm², futa = 400 MPa and fya = 240 MPa: the cap is min(456, 860) = 456 MPa, which does not bind, Vsa = 0.6 × 84.3 × 400 = 20.23 kN and φVbolt = 13.15 kN. A value from a manufacturer or an evaluation report can be given instead as phiVboltOverride.

⚠️ Always pushes a warning that only the anchor steel is checked — ACI318-19, 17.7.2. Concrete breakout (17.7.2) and pryout (17.7.3) depend on edge distance, spacing and embedment and must be checked separately; they often govern near the soffit of a beam.
Bolt requirement

For each plate, with n bolts per row and a transfer length Lt over which the plate force is developed:

s = n · φVbolt / q ≤ 300 mm,   rows = ⌈Lt / s⌉ + 1,   Nq = rows · n
NF = ⌈ Ap fy,p / φVbolt ⌉,   N = max(Nq, NF)the bolts must also develop the plate's full yield force

The result says which governs (governedBy). For the example above with 2 bolts per row over 1500 mm: s = 2 × 13.15/0.2203 = 119.4 mm, rows = ⌈1500/119.4⌉ + 1 = 14, Nq = 28, NF = ⌈300/13.15⌉ = 23, so 28 bolts at 119.4 mm (shear flow governs). A pitch above 300 mm is capped with a warning, and a pitch below 4da gets a warning citing ACI318-19, 17.9.2.

Side plates for shear rc-beam-side-plate-shear.ts

Plates of thickness tp on one or both faces (ns = 1 or 2) of the web engage a depth dp ≤ d. They are continuous, or strips of width wp at pitch s inclined at α to the beam axis. Both layouts share one coverage ratio:

ρc = wp / s for strips,   ρc = 1 and α = 90° for a continuous plate

The strength before strengthening is φVc + φVs from the rc functions (φVs = 0 without stirrups). Two models give the plate contribution Vp; compareSidePlateShearCapacity runs both and reports the lower result as governing.

Model 1: supplementary web AISC 360 G2.1

The plate is a steel web that reaches the von Mises shear yield stress τy = 0.6fy:

Vp = ns · 0.6 fy · tp · dp · ρc

A web panel yields in shear before it buckles when dp/tp ≤ 2.24√(Es/fy) (AISC 360-22, G2.1(a)). The concrete restrains the plate on one face, so a more slender plate gets a warning citing AISC360-22, G2.1 rather than a reduced strength. For strips, ρc = wp/s is an approximation: a narrow strip works more as a tie than as a web panel, which is the second model.

Model 2: tension ties ACI 440.2R-17 §11.4

The plate is bonded shear reinforcement crossing a 45° crack, as in ACI 440.2R-17 11.4 with steel in place of FRP:

Vp = ψf · ns · tp wp ffe (sin α + cos α) dp / s,   ffe = min( fy, 0.004 Es ),   ψf = 0.85

The strain cap of 0.004 keeps the crack tight enough for aggregate interlock. For structural steel 0.004Es = 800 MPa, above any normal fy, so the cap rarely binds and the tie reaches yield. For a continuous plate (wp = s, α = 90°) this model therefore usually gives more than the web model.

Limits, anchorage and the result
  1. Web crushing. The web can host at most Vs + Vp = 0.66√f′c bw d (ACI 318-19 22.5.1.2; ACI 440.2R-17 Eq. 11.4.3). Vp is cut to the headroom 0.66√f′c bw d − Vs, with a warning. If the existing stirrups already use it all, RCDesignError 206 is thrown, since no plate force can be developed.
  2. Strip spacing. s must not exceed min(d/2, 600 mm), or min(d/4, 300 mm) once Vs + Vp > 0.33√f′c bw d (ACI 318-19 9.7.6.2.2), so that a diagonal crack cannot pass between strips.
  3. Anchorage (optional). The bolt rows crossing the 45° crack number max(1, ⌊dp / row spacing⌋), and φVanch = ns · rows · bolts per row · φVbolt. Without anchorage a warning says the plate is assumed fully developed.
  4. Result. φVp = min(0.75 Vp,eff, φVanch) and φVn,after = φVn,before + φVp. governedBy is plateCapacity, webCrushing or anchorage.
Worked example: strips
TypeScript
compareSidePlateShearCapacity(
  { fc_: 28, bw: 300, d: 440, Av: 157, fyt: 400, s: 200 }, // MPa, mm, mm²
  { fy: 250, thickness: 3, depth: 400, configuration: "strips", width: 60, spacing: 200,
    anchorage: { bolt: { Ase: 84.3, futa: 400, fya: 240, diameter: 12 }, boltsPerRow: 3, rowSpacing: 200 } },
);
// before: phiVn 192.68 kN (phiVc 89.06 + phiVs 103.62)
// webYielding: Vp 108 kN → phiVp 81.00, phiVn 273.68, governedBy "plateCapacity"
// tensionTie:  Vp 153 kN → phiVp 114.75, phiVn 307.43, governedBy "plateCapacity"
// anchorage:   2 rows × 3 bolts × 2 faces × 13.15 = 157.80 kN (does not govern)
// governing:   { method: "webYielding", phiVn: 273.68, phiVp: 81 }

By hand: ρc = 60/200 = 0.3; web model Vp = 2 × 150 × 3 × 400 × 0.3 = 108 kN; tie model Vp = 0.85 × 2 × 3 × 60 × 250 × 1 × 400/200 = 153 kN. Both are below the headroom 461.0 − 138.16 = 322.84 kN. This example also returns two warnings worth reading: dp/tp = 133.3 > 63.4 (shear buckling may precede yielding), and the strip pitch of 200 mm exceeds 110 mm, because Vs + Vp = 246.16 kN passes 0.33√f′c bw d = 230.5 kN and halves the allowed spacing to d/4.

The same beam with continuous 6 mm plates shows the web-crushing limit: the web model gives Vp = 720 kN and the tie model 1020 kN, both cut to the headroom of 322.84 kN, so φVp = 242.13 kN and φVn = 434.81 kN, governedBy: "webCrushing".

⚠️ Throws RCDesignError 205 for inconsistent side-plate geometry, 206 when the existing Vs leaves no headroom, 202 for an invalid bolt capacity.

Limitations
  • Debonding and peeling at the plate ends, and the anchorage of the plate beyond the point where it is needed, are not checked. They are the most common failure modes of plated beams.
  • Compression plates are not checked for buckling between bolts.
  • Concrete breakout and pryout of the anchors are not evaluated.
  • Initial strain from the load on the beam when it is strengthened is ignored; the existing steel may yield before the plate reaches its yield stress.
  • Eccentricity. A side plate on one face loads the section eccentrically; the torsion is not captured, only warned about.
  • Fire and corrosion protection of the plates and the adhesive are outside the calculation.
Points for review
  • ACI 440.2R-17 subsections. The subsections that carry the 0.004 effective strain cap and ψf = 0.85 are not yet confirmed against the guide, so they are cited as §11.4 only (a TODO in rc-beam-side-plate-shear.ts). The FRP guide's own strain limits depend on the wrapping scheme and bond-reduction factors; whether they carry over to steel plates is itself a question for review.
  • Strain in the plate. The flexural model counts the bottom plate at fy,p, and the tension-control check is made at the combined centroid dnew. Whether the existing bars and the plate both reach yield is not checked layer by layer, and the plate's own Ep is not used in flexure (only in the interface model).
  • Equivalent area by strength. Scaling the plate area by fy,p/fy keeps the force right but places the plate's compression or tension at the rebar's yield strain. A strain-compatibility model with each steel at its own E and fy would be more general.
  • Bolt pitch limit. The 300 mm maximum pitch is detailing practice, not a code value; projects may need their own limit.
  • Anchorage crack geometry. The count of bolt rows crossing the crack assumes its horizontal projection is dp (a 45° crack).
