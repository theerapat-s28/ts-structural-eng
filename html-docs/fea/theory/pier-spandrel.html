Piers & Spandrels — Theory Notes

How the engine sums the forces of the shell elements and members of a wall pier or spandrel on cuts across it into one resultant per cut, with the sign conventions and the checks against statics.

Overview

A shear wall analysed with shell elements gives forces per unit width at the corners of every element. Walls are designed section by section: the axial force, shears and moments on a horizontal cut through a pier (a wall, a group of wall legs, a core, with any columns in it), and the shear and end moments of a spandrel (a coupling beam over an opening). The user groups the areas and members into piers and spandrels; the engine cuts across each group and reports one resultant per cut, about the centroid of the cut section.

ℹ️ The basis is the equilibrium of the finite element model, not a code provision FEM. Pier and spandrel forces are analysis demands only; wall design (ACI 318-19 11.5, 18.10, 18.10.7) is not part of them. Every number on this page comes from the library's tests (tests/fea/analysis/wall-forces.test.ts) or from running the shown inputs through analyze.

The resultant is the force and moment the rest of the structure exerts on the part on the negative side of the cut (below a pier cut, left of a spandrel cut), across the face whose outward normal is +1. In components along 1, 2, 3 this is P, V2, V3 and T, M2, M3: exactly the signs of a member's internal forces (MemberStation) with the same local axes. So:

  • P is positive in tension;
  • a pier reads like a column of the same orientation: a vertical column has local 2 = +X and 3 = +Y, which is a pier's default with no areas;
  • a spandrel reads like a beam with local 2 up: M3 is positive sagging, and with no load on it M3(x) = M3(0) − x·V2, so V2 = (M3,left − M3,right) / L.
Where the cuts are

Write s = e1 · x for the coordinate of a point along axis 1 (the elevation z for a pier).

1
Pier levels. The smallest and largest s of every area (over its mesh) and of every member: the bottom and top of each lift. Levels closer than the tolerance (WALL_CUT_TOLERANCE × the pier's height) are one level.
2
Faces. At each level, a "top" cut if something of the pier ends there from below, and a "bottom" cut if something starts there going up. A floor level between two lifts has both, the top first.
3
Spandrel ends. "left" at the smallest s, "right" at the largest.
4
Extra cuts from cuts, not at a level already cut, as "cut": taken from the negative side.
5
Mesh lines. No shell element of the group may have corners on both sides of any cut: a cut must run along element edges. Validation reports each such area and cut (331).

Why a "top" and a "bottom" at the same level: the nodes there also carry the floor (slab, beams) and any nodal loads. The force just below the floor and just above it differ by what the floor delivers, so both are reported.

Nodal forces

For each shell element, the forces its nodes exert on it are fe = ke ue − fe,load (global axes, 6 per corner): the element stiffness times its corner displacements, minus its own surface loads and self-weight at the corners. Take the group's elements on one side of the cut that reach it. Their equilibrium as one free body says the force across the cut is the sum of fe at their corners on the cut:

Force and moment about the centroid c
F = σ · Σ fe,i                    // i: corners on the cut of the elements on its side
M = σ · Σ ( me,i + (xi − c) × fe,i )

σ = +1 from the negative side ("top", "right", "cut")
σ = −1 from the positive side ("bottom", "left")
P = F·e1, V2 = F·e2, V3 = F·e3, T = M·e1, M2 = M·e2, M3 = M·e3

From the positive side, the sum is the force on the part above (or right), and the force on the part below is its opposite. This is exact equilibrium of the analysed model, whatever the mesh: a base cut always equals the loads above it. It uses each element's full nodal forces, drilling moments included, so the membrane's imperfect stress field does not enter, unlike integrating stresses along the cut (strips).

Members
  • Ending on the cut (at a node there, e.g. a column split at the wall's mesh nodes): its piece on the cut's side adds its end force (the force the node exerts on the piece, rotated to global axes), with the same σ.
  • Crossing the cut between its nodes: it adds its internal force at the crossing, from the free body of the piece from its start, as its diagram (point loads exactly at the crossing not yet included), with sign +1 when the piece starts on the negative side and −1 otherwise.
  • Lying in the cut plane (both ends on it): ignored. Validation rejects horizontal pier members and spandrel members normal to axis 1.
Section & centroid

Each area with elements on the cut's side gives one leg: the segment through its corners on the cut, along e1 × n (n the area's normal), from the smallest to the largest coordinate, oriented up (spandrel) or toward +X (+Y if parallel to Y). Each member on the cut gives a point with its section area A. Separate legs (walls either side of a door) are simply more legs: they are summed together.

Centroid
c = ( Σ Lj tj xmid,j + Σ Ak xk ) / ( Σ Lj tj + Σ Ak )

The centroid is geometric (not weighted by E), and it is the cut's own: the top and bottom of a lift, or cuts through a changing section, can have different centroids. A cut touched only at element corners has no length; its centroid is the mean of those points.

Combinations

Every term is linear in the displacements, the element loads and the member forces, which are linear in the loads; the cuts and centroids do not depend on the loads. A combination's state is the factored sum of its cases' states, so its pier and spandrel forces are the factored sums of theirs, exactly. envelope.piers and envelope.spandrels give the max and min of each resultant at every cut over all combinations, with the governing combination.

The tests also check that:

  • two separate legs in one pier give the sum of the two single-leg piers moved to the common centroid;
  • a boundary column adds its A to the section and its forces to the cut, which still matches statics;
  • a pier made only of a column gives the column's own diagram values, at its ends and at a cut between its nodes;
  • angle = 90° turns the base shear into V3 = −100 kN and the moment into M2 = 600 kN·m;
  • reverse swaps a spandrel's ends;
  • combinations superpose, and the envelope names the governing combination at each cut;
  • validation (331) catches each wrong input, including lifts broken at different elevations (3.0 m and 3.2 m).
Limitations
  • Cuts follow the mesh. A cut must lie on element edges of every area it meets. Lifts of different legs broken at different elevations need those areas divided; the engine does not add mesh lines.
  • Element nodal forces, not stresses. The resultant is exact for the model, but how it is shared along the section (the stress distribution) is not reported; use shellForces for that.
  • One group per kind. An area is in at most one pier, so the same wall cannot be reported both as separate legs and as one pier in one analysis.
  • Vertical piers, planar spandrels. Sloped walls are not piers; a spandrel lies in one vertical plane.
  • No design. Boundary elements, P–M interaction and coupling-beam detailing are not part of these results.
