Strip Forces — Theory Notes

How the engine turns the forces of the shell elements under a design strip into the bending moment, shear, torsion and axial force on cuts across the strip, with the sign conventions and the checks against statics.

⚠️ A calculation aid, not a substitute for engineering judgement. Results must be checked and accepted by the qualified engineer responsible for the design. Scope, assumptions and limitations →
Overview

A slab analysed with shell elements gives forces per unit width at the corners of every element (shellForces: F11 … V23 in kN/m and kN·m/m). Slabs are designed in strips: a column strip or middle strip of a given width, whose total moment and shear at each section along it are what the reinforcement and the shear check need. A design strip is a polyline drawn over the slab with a width on each side. At stations along it the engine cuts across the strip and integrates the shell forces along each cut.

ℹ️ The basis is the finite element method and plate theory, not a code provision FEM. Strip forces are the analysis demands only; they do not apply Wood–Armer or any code rule for distributing moments. Every number on this page comes from the library's tests (tests/fea/shell/strip-forces.test.ts) or from running the shown inputs through analyze.

Axes & signs

Each segment of the polyline, from pk to pk+1, has its own axes:

n = normal / |normal|     a = d − (d·n)n|d − (d·n)n|,   d = pk+1 − pk     b = n × a axis 1 = a (along), 2 = b (across, to the left), 3 = n

The six resultants are the force and moment that the part of the strip ahead of the cut applies to the part behind it, on the face whose outward normal is +1, as for a member's internal forces (N, V2, M3 of memberDiagrams):

Steps shell/post/strip-forces.ts
1
Find the shells in the strip's plane — every element whose corners are within STRIP_PLANE_TOLERANCE of the plane.
2
Place the stations along each segment, and at each one the cut from −wR to +wL.
3
Cut the elements: clip the cut to each element, giving each element's piece of it.
4
Evaluate each element's force field at Gauss points on its piece, rotate it to the strip axes and add it up.
5
Present: kN and kN·m, per-width values, extremes along the strip; for combinations, the same from the combined shell forces, and the envelope.

Steps 1–4 depend only on the geometry. They run once per analysis and give, for each station, fixed coefficients that turn the elements' raw corner forces into the six resultants. Each load case and combination then only multiplies those coefficients by its own shell forces.

Shells in the plane

The strip plane passes through points[0] with normal n. An analysed element (after meshing) belongs to the strip when each of its four corners xi satisfies

|(xi − p0) · n| ≤ τ · hmax τ = STRIP_PLANE_TOLERANCE = 10⁻³, hmax = the element's longest side (1 mm on a 1 m element)

Walls, beams' flanges in other planes and slabs at other levels are ignored, so a strip on a floor never picks up the walls below it. A slab with a fall (a ramp) is not flat; give the strip the ramp's normal or model the ramp as its own plane.

Each element remembers the sign σ = sign(e3 · n) of its own local 3 against the strip's normal. Moments and transverse shear are defined about local 3 (sagging = tension on the −3 face), so they are multiplied by σ: a slab drawn clockwise from above (local 3 down) gives the same strip moments as one drawn anticlockwise.

Stations

A segment of in-plane length Lk is divided into nk equal parts:

nk = max(1, ⌈Lk / maxSpacing⌉)   or   nk = 20 without maxSpacing     cj = pk + (j / nk)(pk+1 − pk),   j = 0 … nk
  • x is the distance along the centreline from points[0], measured in the plane.
  • Every polyline point is a station. A bend has two stations at the same x: the end of the incoming segment, cut normal to it, and the start of the outgoing one, cut normal to that (segment tells them apart). A straight continuation gives two equal stations.
  • The cut at station c is the line X(s) = c + s·b, −wR ≤ s ≤ wL.
Cutting the elements

Each element in the plane is a convex quadrilateral (checked by validation, error 326) with corners (xi, yi) in its own local 1–2 coordinates, anticlockwise about its local 3. The cut is written in those coordinates as c′ + s d′, where c′ = ((c − o)·e1, (c − o)·e2), d′ = (b·e1, b·e2) and o is the element's centroid. It is clipped against the four edges (Cyrus–Beck): edge i, from corner i to i + 1, with inward unit normal mi, keeps the part where

mi · (c′ − xi) + s (mi · d′) ≥ 0

The intersection of the four conditions with −wR ≤ s ≤ wL is the element's piece [sfrom, sto] of the cut. A cut running along an element edge (within 10⁻⁶ × the element size) counts as inside it.

All the pieces' end points are sorted into sub-intervals of the cut. A sub-interval covered by k elements gives each of them the weight 1/k. On an ordinary cut k = 1; a cut lying exactly on a mesh line, which is common since stations often fall on mesh lines, takes the mean of the elements on both sides.

Force field in an element

The shell reports eight forces per corner (see the shell element notes): F11, F22, F12 (N/mm), M11, M22, M12 (N·mm/mm) and V13, V23 (N/mm). F and M are computed at the 2×2 Gauss points and extrapolated bilinearly to the corners, so interpolating the corner values bilinearly reproduces exactly the field through the Gauss points:

f(ξ, η) = Σi=1…4 Ni(ξ, η) fi,    Ni = ¼(1 + ξiξ)(1 + ηiη) f = any of F11 … M12; fi its value at corner i

The point of the cut, in the element's coordinates (x, y), is mapped back to (ξ, η) by Newton's method on the bilinear map x(ξ, η) = Σ Ni xi (one step for a parallelogram, a few for a distorted quad). Each element's field is used on its own: no averaging between elements, as decided for this feature. The corner V13, V23 are not used; the shear is taken from the moment field's derivative instead, see Shear & torsion.

Rotation to the strip axes

The element's forces are tensors in its local axes e1, e2. With the direction cosines

ca1 = a·e1,   ca2 = a·e2,   cb1 = b·e1,   cb2 = b·e2

the components on the cut (whose normal is a) are, per unit length of the cut:

Faa = ca1² F11 + ca2² F22 + 2 ca1ca2 F12 normal membrane force
Fba = cb1ca1 F11 + cb2ca2 F22 + (cb1ca2 + cb2ca1) F12 in-plane shear
Maa = σ (ca1² M11 + ca2² M22 + 2 ca1ca2 M12) bending moment, sagging about n
mab = σ (cb1ca1 M11 + cb2ca2 M22 + (cb1ca2 + cb2ca1) M12) twisting moment

σ = ±1 is the sign of the element's local 3 against n (see Shells in the plane). Membrane forces do not depend on which face is up, so they take no σ. The strip direction a may be at any angle to the element axes; the rotation also handles the shell's angle.

Resultants

With s the offset along b from the cut's centre on the centreline, the resultants on the +1 face are the integrals along the covered part of the cut:

P = ∫ Faa ds     Vp = ∫ Fba ds     M = ∫ Maa ds     Mp = −∫ s Faa ds
V = −∫ ∂Maa∂a ds     T = ∫ ( 2 mab − s ∂Maa∂a ) ds V and T: see Shear & torsion

On each element's piece [u, v] of the cut, the integral is a 4-point Gauss–Legendre rule (points tg, weights wg):

∫uv g(s) ds ≈ v − u2 Σg=1…4 wg · g(u + v2 + v − u2 tg)    × 1/k on overlapping pieces

Along a straight cut through a parallelogram, ξ and η are linear in s, so the field is quadratic in s and the integrands are at most cubic: the rule is exact. On distorted quads it is accurate to round-off for ordinary mesh shapes.

Moment sign of Mp. The traction Faa at offset s·b has a moment (sb) × (Faaa) = −s Faa n about the centre, hence the minus sign: tension on the right side (s < 0) gives a positive Mp.

Per width. perWidth divides each resultant by the covered width of the cut (in m), giving kN/m and kN·m/m: the average over the strip, which is what a strip design per metre uses. Where width is 0 the per-width values are 0.

Shear & torsion

The plate is a thin (Kirchhoff) plate. Its transverse shear is not an independent field: it follows from moment equilibrium,

Qa = −( ∂Maa∂a + ∂mab∂b ) the shell's V13 = −(∂M11/∂x₁ + ∂M12/∂x₂), turned to the strip axes

Integrating Qa alone along a cut does not give the shear that statics requires. In Kirchhoff theory a twisting moment on a free edge is statically equivalent to a shear force along that edge plus concentrated forces mab at its ends (Kirchhoff's edge forces, the same effect as the corner forces 2Mxy of a rectangular plate). A cut's ends are such edges: the slab's free edges, openings and the edges of the strip itself. In a real slab, a narrow boundary layer of transverse shear, about a slab thickness wide, carries these forces; thin-plate theory folds it into the twisting moment.

Adding the edge forces mab at the two ends of the cut, the ∂mab/∂b term integrates out exactly:

V = ∫ Qa ds + mab(wL) − mab(−wR) = −∫ ∂Maa∂a ds

The moment about a of the same system (couples mab, shear Qa at lever s, end forces at levers wL and −wR), integrated by parts:

T = ∫ (mab + s Qa) ds + [ s mab ]−wRwL = ∫ ( 2 mab − s ∂Maa∂a ) ds

The factor 2 is the classical result for the torsion of a thin plate strip: half the torque is carried by the twisting moments and half by the edge shears (Timoshenko & Woinowsky-Krieger). The engine applies both identities within each element's piece of the cut, so the twisting moment's jumps between elements, which are discretisation error, cancel too. As a result:

  • V = −dM/dx along a straight strip of constant width, exactly, for the integrated field, because ∫ ∂Maa/∂a ds = d/dx ∫ Maa ds.
  • A full-width cut reproduces statics: V and T balance the loads beyond the cut (see Verification).
ℹ️ Why this matters. On the 6 m × 2 m simply supported slab (ν = 0.2), integrating Qa alone gave −27.67 kN at quarter span on every mesh tried (12 × 4 to 48 × 8), where statics gives −30 kN: 8 % low, from the twisting moments at the free edges. Adding the edge forces only at the cut's two ends gave −31.7 to −32.0 kN, because the element-by-element twisting moment also jumps between elements. The element-wise form above gives −30.000 kN on all meshes.
Recovery along the strip

∂Maa/∂a comes from the bilinear moment field, so it is constant along 1 within an element. Taken straight from the elements, V would be a stair with one step per element, stopping half an element short of a support. Each step is exact at its element's centre: a full-width cut through the centre of an element balances the loads beyond it. So V and T are taken at the element centres and joined by straight lines.

1
Mesh lines. The corners of the elements under the band give the lines across a segment. Between two lines is one cell; the cut through its centre gives the value there.
2
Jumps. A line has a jump when a node of it in the band has a support, a member, a nodal load or a shell off the strip's plane (a wall). Three or more such lines in a row are something lying along the strip, such as a beam, which takes load all along it with no jump, so they mark none.
3
Station. V and T at a station come from the straight line through the centres of its cell and the next cell, or, where that is across a jump or covers a different width, the cell on the other side, extended. So the shear reaches a support, free edge or slab end.
4
On a jump. A station at the end of a segment reads its own side. To get both sides of a column, put a strip point there: the end of one segment and the start of the next have the two values. A station inside a segment that falls exactly on a jump takes the mean of the two sides.

M, P, Vp and Mp are unchanged. V = −dM/dx holds at the element centres. The weights are fixed, so each station's V and T stay linear in the raw corner forces, and combinations superpose exactly.

Coverage & gaps

width is the length of the cut that lies on elements in the plane. It is less than wL + wR where the cut crosses an opening, runs past a slab edge, or the strip is wider than the slab. The resultants then integrate only the covered parts, and per-width values use the covered width. Each covered piece of the cut ends at an edge, and the Kirchhoff edge forces apply there (they are already included element by element).

⚠️ Pushes one warning per strip to result.warnings when any of its stations is not fully on shells (giving their count), or crosses no shell element in its plane when it misses them all — Strip forces, shell coverage.
Combinations & envelope

Every step is linear in the elements' raw corner forces, which are linear in the loads. The engine stores, for each station, the coefficients that map those 32 numbers per element (8 forces × 4 corners) to the six resultants. A combination's strip forces come from its combined (unrounded) shell forces through the same coefficients, so they equal the factored sum of the case values exactly, and rounding happens only on presentation.

envelope.strips gives, at every station, the max and min of each resultant and of its per-width value over all combinations, with the governing combination, which is the strip's design envelope diagram. It also gives the max and min of each resultant over the whole strip, with the station's x.

The tests also check that:

  • on a flat plate, a full-width cut gives each side of a column the shear statics requires, the jump equals the column line's reactions, and V is a straight line along a span;
  • a beam along the strip does not count as a jump, and the shear is not carried across a change in covered width;
  • reversing the strip keeps M and flips V;
  • a slab whose corners run clockwise (local 3 down) gives the same signs;
  • a shell angle of 30° changes no strip force;
  • two half-width strips add up to the full-width strip in M and V;
  • a strip wider than the slab reports width = 2000 mm, the same M, and a warning;
  • a strip in another plane picks up nothing and warns;
  • combinations superpose, and the envelope names the governing combination at each station.

In the half strip, T is not zero: near the supports, the twisting moments of the anticlastic slab (ν = 0.2) do not cancel over half the width. They cancel over the whole width, where T = 0.

Limitations
  • Shear beside a column. The recovered line is exact for a uniform load across a full-width cut. On part of the width, close to a column, the shear rises faster than a straight line and the value at the column still depends on the mesh: 99.5 kN at 1000 mm against 107.3 kN at 125 mm for a 3 m strip over a flat plate. Check shear at the critical section, d or d/2 from the support face, rather than at the support line.
  • Concentrated loads and columns. Close to a point load or a column the plate field is singular and the shells' moments and shears depend on the mesh; strip values there are as good as the mesh. Refine the mesh around columns.
  • Membrane forces come from the membrane's strain field, which does not satisfy equilibrium exactly on a cut: P, Vp and Mp are within 0.2 % of statics in the cantilever above.
  • Flat strips only. A strip lies in one plane and picks up only the elements in that plane (within 1 mm per metre). It does not follow a folded or curved surface.
  • No Wood–Armer. M is the bending moment about the cut and T the torsion. Designing reinforcement for the twisting moments (e.g. Wood–Armer moments) is not part of strip forces.
  • Thin plate. The shells are Kirchhoff plates, so the shear identities above hold; with thick plates (planned) V and T would be revisited.
